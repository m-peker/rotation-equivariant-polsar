**Fig. 1.** (double column) — `fig1_scene.pdf`

> Flevoland AIRSAR scene (750 × 1024). (a) Pauli RGB composite
> (R = T₂₂, G = T₃₃, B = T₁₁, 2–98 % linear stretch in dB). (b) Reference map,
> 15 land-cover classes, 207 832 labelled pixels. (c) Euclidean distance of each
> labelled pixel to the nearest class boundary, clipped at 15 px. Because the
> scene is composed of small agricultural fields, 54.0 % of labelled pixels lie
> within 7 px of a boundary — i.e. within the radius of the 15 × 15 patch used
> by patch-based classifiers.

**Fig. 2.** (double column) — `fig2_rotation.pdf`

> Overall accuracy under polarimetric rotation of the test data,
> T → U(θ) T Uᵀ. Flevoland, class-balanced budget of 133 labels per class, mean
> of two seeds. (a) Full range: the baseline complex-valued CNN loses 54.6
> percentage points between θ = 0° and θ = 45°, and the published CV-MsAtViT
> architecture — which is the stronger of the two on unrotated data — loses 47.8.
> (b) Detail of the rotation-robust models. Rotation augmentation flattens the
> curve but costs clean accuracy; the cost is structural, since neither 3×
> training nor 4× capacity recovers it (Table II). The proposed equivariant
> network is flat to within 0.03 pp and sits 2.53 pp above the augmented
> baseline at 58 % of its parameters.
>
> Regenerated by `fig2_update.py` directly from `exp15_results.json`, so the
> figure cannot drift away from Table I.

**Fig. 3.** (double column) — `fig3_boundary.pdf`

> Boundary-resolved error analysis of the baseline network on Flevoland
> (mean of three seeds, standard 1 % protocol, OA = 97.57 ± 0.31 %).
> (a) Error rate stratified by distance to the nearest class boundary; pixels
> 1–2 px from a boundary are misclassified 14× more often than interior pixels.
> (b) Distribution of errors compared with the distribution of the test
> population: 72.5 % of all errors fall within 2 px of a boundary while only
> 23.1 % of test pixels lie there, a 3.14× enrichment. The residual error of a
> saturated benchmark is therefore almost entirely a boundary phenomenon —
> a fact that aggregate OA hides.

---

**Fig. 4.** (double column) — `fig4_leakage.pdf`

> Spatial leakage in the standard evaluation protocol, shown on a 200 × 200
> crop of Flevoland. (a) Random 1 % pixel split: red marks test pixels whose
> 15 × 15 patch overlaps at least one training patch. Over the full scene this
> is 173 344 of 205 754 test pixels (84.2 %). (b) The proposed block + buffer
> partition (96 px blocks, 8 px buffer) yields zero overlap, verified by
> morphological dilation of the training mask with the patch footprint; a
> class-aware block assignment guarantees all 15 classes appear in the training
> partition.

**Fig. 5.** (single column) — `fig5_equivariance.pdf`

> Numerical verification of equivariance. Relative deviation
> ‖f(U(θ)x) − f(x)‖∞ ⁄ ‖f(x)‖∞ of the network output under polarimetric
> rotation, float64, measured on real Flevoland patches with the
> trace-normalised representation of Section III-C. The discrete
> group-convolutional network is exact only on the group grid θ = m·π/N
> (dotted lines), where it reaches 5.0·10⁻¹⁶, and deviates by up to 3.6·10⁻²
> between grid points. The Fourier readout is a continuous function of the group
> axis where the maximum is not, which reduces the off-grid deviation by an order
> of magnitude (3.8·10⁻³) without removing it. The steerable network, operating
> directly in the circular-harmonic basis, stays at 5.9·10⁻¹⁵ for arbitrary
> angles — six orders of magnitude lower, at the level of float64 round-off.
>
> Measured and plotted by `fig5_update.py`; raw values in `fig5_data.json`.

**Fig. 6.** (double column) — `fig6_maps.pdf`

> Full-scene classification maps of Flevoland (768 000 pixels) under
> polarimetric rotation of the input. Rows: prediction at θ = 0°, prediction at
> θ = 45°, and the pixels whose predicted label changed between the two.
> Columns: baseline CV-CNN, baseline trained with rotation augmentation, and the
> proposed steerable equivariant network, all with the same 133 labels per class.
> The baseline collapses from 97.46 % to 42.74 % and relabels 57.9 % of the
> scene. Augmentation holds accuracy (94.83 % → 94.74 %) but its invariance is
> only approximate: 5.1 % of pixels still change, concentrated along class
> boundaries. The steerable network returns bitwise identical maps —
> **not a single pixel of 768 000 changes** — at 95.68 %, i.e. 0.85 points above
> the augmented baseline, because its invariance is a property of the
> architecture rather than something learned from data.
